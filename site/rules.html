Supreme Court of New South Wales
Practice Note SC Gen 23 (NSW Supreme Court): Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence
Official text · issued 2025-01-28		Legal practitioners and unrepresented parties; deponents of affidavits, witness statements and character references; expert witnesses (practitioners must draw the note to experts' attention).Affidavits, witness statements and character references must state that Gen AI was not used to generate their content. Where Gen AI helped prepare written submissions, the author must verify the references in the body of the submissions. An expert given leave must disclose which parts used Gen AI and keep a record of how.Banned: generating the content of affidavits, witness statements, character references or other evidentiary material; altering or embellishing a witness's evidence; drafting expert reports without prior leave. Suppressed, Harman-protected, subpoenaed or statutorily protected material must not be entered into Gen AI unless strict confidentiality conditions are met. Expressly permitted: chronologies, indexes, witness lists, summaries, draft submissions (subject to verification).If Gen AI was used for submissions, every citation, authority and legislative reference must be verified to exist, be accurate and be relevant, and references to evidence verified too. Verification must not be done solely with a Gen AI tool.No specific sanction is stated. Using Gen AI does not qualify or absolve the author's professional or ethical obligations.who: 7, 24; disclosure: 13, 16, 22; uses: 9A, 9B, 10, 12, 20; verification: 16, 17; consequences: 18
Supreme Court of Victoria
Practice Note SC Gen 25 (Victorian Supreme Court): The Use of Artificial Intelligence by Court Users
Official text · issued 2026-05-14		All Court users: lawyers, litigants (represented or self-represented), and lay and expert witnesses.No general duty to disclose AI use, but users must not mislead anyone about the nature of AI-assisted work, must be prepared to identify the portions produced with AI and explain how they were verified, and the Court may direct further information.Cautioned: confidential or sensitive information (including suppressed material) should not be entered into public AI tools; particular caution for affidavits, witness statements and expert reports, which must reflect the person's own knowledge and words.AI content must be verified with meaningful human control. Users should verify that cases, legislation, textbooks and articles exist and stand for the propositions attributed to them, and that quotes are accurate, using a source known to be accurate. Asking one AI tool to confirm another's output is not verification.Filing inaccurate documents could result in costs orders. Lawyers relying on unverified AI outputs may be referred to the Victorian Legal Services Board and Commissioner and face personal costs orders.who: 2.2, 3.1; disclosure: 6.2, 7.5, 7.6; uses: 6.8, 6.10, 6.11, 6.12; verification: 7.1, 7.4; consequences: 6.4, 6.7
Supreme Court of Victoria
Practice Note SC Gen 26 (Victorian Supreme Court): Practice Note for Self-Represented Litigants (AI paragraphs only)
Official text · issued 2026-09-14		Self-represented litigants in civil proceedings in the Trial Division, Court of Appeal and Costs Court.None stated.Self-represented litigants should not use AI programs for legal advice.All legal information and advice obtained from AI programs must be checked; Law Library Victoria provides tools to verify citations in AI-generated documents.Giving the Court false information can lead to serious trouble, and reliance on an AI program is not an excuse.who: 2.2; uses: 8.12; verification: 8.12, 8.7; consequences: 8.12
Supreme Court of Queensland
Practice Direction 5 of 2025 (Queensland Supreme Court): Accuracy of References in Submissions
Official text · issued 2025-09-24		The responsible person for written or oral submissions: the individual legal practitioner (not just the firm), or a self-represented litigant.No duty to disclose AI use. The responsible person must be named at the end of written submissions; naming the firm is not enough. Naming a practitioner tells the Court they have done the verification.No use is banned. The direction targets the risk that AI produces non-existent sources or unchecked reformulations.Practitioners must verify the accuracy and relevance of every reference to legislation, authorities or other sources, in written and oral submissions. Self-represented litigants must endeavour to ensure the accuracy of their references.Practitioners: possible referral to the Legal Services Commissioner and/or being required to show cause why a personal costs order should not be made. Parties relying on non-existent references: possible adjournment and adverse costs order.who: 5, 6, 16; disclosure: 6, 10; uses: 2; verification: 9, 11, 16; consequences: 14, 17

Supreme Court of Western Australia
Consolidated Practice Directions PD 9.21 (WA Supreme Court): Guidelines for the use of generative AI
Official text · issued 2025-12-10		Everyone involved in proceedings: lawyers, non-lawyers, self-represented litigants in civil and criminal proceedings, and expert witnesses.AI use should be disclosed to other parties and the Court when directed or otherwise necessary or appropriate; experts should consider what disclosures to make.Banned: using generative AI in a way that may mislead about the work done or how a document was produced; AI content substituting a witness's own voice in affidavits and witness statements. Cautioned: confidential, suppressed or privileged information should not go into public or unsecured tools; expert reports must comply with the Harmonised Expert Witness Code.AI-generated content relied on in proceedings must be verified by a human who takes legal responsibility for the document. Practitioners must verify that documents contain nothing misleading and that every factual and legal contention has a proper basis.No specific sanction is stated. Those who sign, file or rely on a document are responsible for its accuracy and omissions, and having used AI is not an adequate response to errors.who: 1; disclosure: 11, 12; uses: 10, 11, 12; verification: 7, 8; consequences: 7
