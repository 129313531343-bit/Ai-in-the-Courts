How the tracker is built and checked

The starting point is the Australian rows of Damien Charlotin's AI Hallucination Cases database. Each judgment's text is extracted locally, with publisher headers and footers removed. Claude (Anthropic) is asked to quote the passages on the AI error, the impugned citations, consequences, referrals and practice notes cited, and every quote is then checked by script against the judgment text, character for character, with its paragraph number. A second step proposes field values from those verified quotes only, and any value without a supporting quote is discarded.

Every row then goes through automatic checks: the citation matches the judgment's header, titles and impugned citations are copied exactly, characterisations have textual support, paragraph counts match the court's own certificate, and the row is compared with Charlotin's coding. These checks prove that each value rests on real text at the stated paragraph. They cannot prove the text was read correctly, so rows that pass are labelled auto-verified. A random sample of 10 rows, fixed before any extraction, was checked by hand against the official judgment and labelled spot-checked. Rows the checks flag are labelled awaiting review until a person has read them.

Sources and a pending question

Most judgment copies in Charlotin's database were printed from AustLII. Our code never accessed AustLII, but the text of those copies (with AustLII's headers removed) was processed by the AI model. We are asking AustLII whether this use is acceptable. Until it replies, the details the AI extracted from those copies are held back. Rows checked by hand against the judgment, facts read by script (citation, court, date) and Charlotin's own coding are shown, as are the fake citations Luc read against the judgment in 20 of the held-back rows.

Privacy

The tracker records only what each judgment says. Self-represented parties can be filtered but not searched by name, and their case titles are not shown on the tracker page. Family law matters use only the court's anonymised title (Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) s 114Q).

Corrections and takedown

Email ai.courts.corrections@proton.me. Requests are answered within 7 days, and every change is logged in the changelog. If you are a self-represented party named in a decision, you can ask for identifying detail to be removed even where the entry is accurate.
